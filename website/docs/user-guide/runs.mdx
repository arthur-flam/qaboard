The **Runs** page lists a project's runs whatever their commit, by experiment: where your trainings from this week
are, which run of a sweep is the best and what it used, how today's run compares with Tuesday's. Projects whose code
[creates runs](/docs/log-from-code) (a training loop, a notebook) have it in the sidebar, under **History**.

:::caution Experimental
The Runs page is new, like `qaboard.start_run()`: its layout, its columns and the options in its address may change.
:::

## Find your experiment {#experiments}
On the left, the project's **experiments** form a tree: the names your code gives them, folded by their slashes.
`detector/lr-sweep` is under `detector`, `detector/lr-sweep/warmup` under `detector/lr-sweep`. Each level says how many
runs it has, how many run now (blue) and failed (red). Click a level to see its runs **and those of every
experiment under it**; **All runs** shows everything.

- **Everyone's runs are one experiment.** Alice's and Bob's runs of `detector/lr-sweep` are in the same level, whoever
  ran them: the **Who** column and the **Yours** / **Everyone** switch tell them apart.
- **Every level has its address**: copy it from the level's **⋯** menu, or with the link button at the top right,
  to share exactly what you see.
- **The time window** (*Last 30 days* by default) keeps the page fast on big projects: pick a longer one to find
  older experiments.
- **The CI's runs are hidden**: experiments are what people run. Turn off **Hide the CI** to see them too, over at
  most 90 days.

Name experiments `<area>/<question>`, like `backbone/lr-sweep` or `quantization/int8-calibration`, with seeds and
trials in the configurations, not in the name: see [name your experiments](/docs/log-from-code#experiments).

## See how the trainings went {#plots}
Above the table, **Logged series** plots what each run logged as it ran (`run.log()`), one line per run in the color
of its row, one plot per series: the validation metrics first, then the training ones, the system's last. As in a
batch's Summary, smooth the curves, switch to a log scale, plot by step or by time, and filter the series by name.

The table drives the plots:
- **Hover a row** and its curves come forward in every plot, the others fade.
- **The eye** hides a run from the plots, and grays out its row (<kbd>h</kbd> on the focused row). The eye in the
  header shows or hides them all.
- **Tick rows** to plot only them (<kbd>Space</kbd> on the focused row). Their best values are those in bold.
  **Plot every run shown** brings back the others.

**Group by** a parameter (e.g. `lr`) gives each of its values a color, and splits the table in groups. Pick
**Mean and range** to draw each group as one line, the mean of its runs, with a band from the lowest to the highest:
what a learning rate gives over several seeds, rather than what one seed gave.

<details>
<summary>Which runs are plotted</summary>

The runs started from code, since only they log series, and at most 40 at a time: the first ones of the table, or
the ticked ones. Runs without a curve have a disabled eye. A run that diverged stops where its values became NaN.

</details>

## Pick the best run {#table}
One row per run, the running first, then the latest:

| Column | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Run | What tells it apart from the others: its input when they differ, its configurations and the parameters that vary |
| Experiment | Its experiment under the level you opened, when it's a deeper one |
| Metrics | The project's main metrics, else the validation metrics first. **Metrics** at the top right picks them |
| Parameters | One column per parameter whose value differs between the runs |
| Who, Started, Duration | Who ran it (click the name to see only their runs), when, how long it ran or has been running (**live**) |
| Commit | Its commit, a link to its page. `*` when the code had changes that were not committed |

- **Sort** by any column, e.g. a metric: the first click puts its best value first. Sorting orders the runs loaded.
- **The best value** of each metric is in bold. QA-Board knows which way is better from the metric's definition,
  else guesses it from its name (`loss`, `error`, `mAP`, `accuracy`...): hover the column's name to see which.
  [Declare the metric](/docs/computing-quantitative-metrics) to be sure.
- **Filter** with words that must all match the run's input, configurations, parameters, experiment, who ran it or
  its commit: `lr=0.001`, `resnet -failed`, `id:1043`. **Only running**, **Only failed** and **Hide failed** are one
  click away.

## Open a run, compare two {#open-a-run}
**Click a row** to open the run on the right: its metrics, its curves, its visualizations and files with every
viewer, as on its commit's page. <kbd>Esc</kbd> or the browser's Back closes it.

- **Compare with…** picks another run of the table as the reference: its curves are dashed, its images next to the
  run's, its metrics' changes in percent. Any two runs, whatever their commits and experiments.
- **Tick two rows** and click **Compare the 2 selected** for the same: the second you ticked is the reference.
- **Open on the commit page** goes to the run's commit, filtered to the run (and compared with the other run): its
  logs, its attempts, the batch's other views.

## Read the same runs from Python {#python}
**Python** at the top right gives the code that reads the runs you see, in a notebook or a script:

```python
import qaboard as qb

runs = qb.runs(project="kitefin/detector", experiment="detector/lr-sweep", since=7)
best = max(runs, key=lambda run: run.metrics.get("val/mAP", 0))
print(best.params, best.output_dir, best.series("val/loss")[-1])
```

The page's address works too: `qb.runs("<the page's address>")` gives what it shows, down to the ticked rows, and
`qa runs "<address>" --json` from a shell. See [results from Python](/docs/apis).

## Make it the project's home {#home}
For projects that train, open the project on its runs rather than its commits: in *qaboard.yaml*,

```yaml title="qaboard.yaml"
project:
  home: runs
```

**Runs** then comes first in the sidebar, and the project's page opens on it. The commits list is still one click
away, under it.

## What it doesn't do yet {#limits}
- **Delete an experiment's files** from its level: delete batches from their commit's page, or with the
  [storage rules](/docs/storage/deleting-old-data).
- **Pin a set of runs as a milestone**, to make it a reference with a name and notes.
- **Sort across every run**: sorting orders the runs loaded, the latest first. Open a level, or shorten the time
  window, to sort the runs you care about.
