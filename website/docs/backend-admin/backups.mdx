QA-Board's backups are **database backups only**: a daily `pg_dump` with docker compose
([Backups](/docs/backend-admin/deployment#backups)), or with the Helm chart
([Backups](/docs/backend-admin/kubernetes#backups)). They never include the runs' files, uploaded results, or the
keys the server keeps on disk. To recover from losing the host or the storage, back up the four parts below, and
restore them together.

## What to back up {/* #what-to-back-up */}
| What | Where | Covered by QA-Board's backups? | If you lose it |
|------|-------|-------------------------------|----------------|
| **The database**: projects, commits, batches, runs and their metrics, users, roles, tokens, settings, audit log | PostgreSQL (the `db` volume) | ✅ daily `pg_dump` | Everything that lists or compares results |
| **The results**: runs' outputs and logs, commits' artifacts, exports, series and media logged from code | Your storage (`QABOARD_STORAGE_ROOTS`, the [storage locations](/docs/backend-admin/uploads#storage-locations)), mounted on the server and the runners | ❌ | The files behind every run: images, viewers, logs, diffs. Runs keep their metrics, without files |
| **Uploaded results**: runs sent over HTTP, by `qa upload` or `qaboard.start_run()` | The upload root, by default the `results` volume at `/mnt/qaboard` ([where the files go](/docs/backend-admin/uploads#layout)) | ❌ | Same as the results |
| **The server's data folder** | `QABOARD_DATA_DIR` (default `/var/qaboard`, the `data` volume; the chart's `-data` volume) | ❌ | See below |
| **Secrets and configuration** | `.env`, your site directory, the chart's Secret | ❌ (keep them in your secret store and in git) | Sign-in, the database password, the encryption key |

**The server's data folder** holds:
- `encryption_key` and `secret_key`, **if the server generated them** (you didn't set `QABOARD_ENCRYPTION_KEY` and
  `SECRET_KEY`). Without the encryption key, a restored database has everything but its stored secrets: sign-in
  providers' and integrations' secrets go back to their defaults ([the encryption key](/docs/backend-admin/settings#secrets-and-the-encryption-key)).
  Without `SECRET_KEY`, everyone is signed out.
- `shared/`: the groups of tests saved from **Run Tests / Tuning**. They exist only there.
- Caches it rebuilds by itself: git clones (`git/`), thumbnails, decoded images. No need to back them up.

The other volumes are caches or rebuilt from the images: Cantaloupe's cache, the web app's and docs' bundles, Redis
(caches, and the committers' avatars, learned again). RabbitMQ's volume has the queued Celery tasks and the accounts
added with `rabbitmqctl`: back it up only if you created accounts there.

:::tip[Keys first]
Put `QABOARD_ENCRYPTION_KEY` and `SECRET_KEY` in your secret store before anything else, even when the server
generated them (`qaboard-admin encryption show-key` prints the key). Never keep them in the same place as the
database backups: whoever has both reads the stored secrets.
:::

## Recovery objectives {/* #rpo-rto */}
- **Recovery point (RPO)**: the daily dump loses up to a day of runs, comments and settings. For less, on
  Kubernetes, use CloudNativePG's continuous backups to object storage (point-in-time recovery to the second); with
  docker compose, add your own more frequent `pg_dump`, or archive PostgreSQL's WAL with your backup tool.
  The results' RPO is your storage's: its snapshots or backups.
- **Recovery time (RTO)**: restoring the database takes longer than the dump, since it rebuilds the indexes: time it
  in a drill (see below). The results' RTO is your storage's.
- **High availability is not a backup**: CloudNativePG's replicas copy a mistaken deletion within a second, and a
  NAS's replication copies a deleted folder. Keep backups that a mistake or an attacker can't change.

## Keep copies off the host {/* #off-site */}
QA-Board writes its dumps next to the database, on the same host (compose) or in one volume (the chart), and doesn't
copy them anywhere else. Copy them off yourself, with your backup system:
- At least one copy in another site, or on immutable storage (object lock, WORM), out of reach of the server's
  accounts: ransomware or an admin's mistake on the host must not reach it.
- Watch the copies, not just the dumps: alert when the newest dump is more than a day old or much smaller than the
  previous ones (`ls -lt` of the backups folder, or your backup system's checks). A failed dump leaves no file:
  the backup script never writes a partial one.
- Retention: with docker compose, daily dumps older than `QABOARD_BACKUP_RETENTION_DAYS` (14) are deleted, but never
  the newest 3; the chart deletes dumps older than `backup.keepDays` (14). Keep longer copies (e.g. monthly) in your
  backup system.

## The backups hold sensitive data {/* #sensitive-data */}
A dump has the users' emails and names, their password hashes (accounts with a password), the hashes of tokens and
of connected apps' secrets, IP addresses and browsers in the audit log and the tokens' last use, and the commits'
author emails. Stored secrets are encrypted with the encryption key (not in the dump).
- Encrypt the copies at rest, and restrict who can read them like you restrict the database.
- Deleting a user or their data in QA-Board doesn't delete it from the backups: it's gone when the last backup that
  has it expires. Say so in your retention policy, e.g. for privacy requests.
- The results can hold your projects' data (images, logs, datasets' outputs): back them up with the access controls
  of the projects' data, not the server's.

## Restore the database and the files together {/* #consistency */}
The database and the results are backed up at different times, so after a restore they rarely match. Restore both,
then reconcile:
- **Runs in the database whose files are gone** (the files are older than the database): their metrics show,
  their files don't. [Purge runs whose files are gone](/docs/backend-admin/storage#purge-runs-whose-files-are-gone)
  removes them from the database. It never purges when the storage looks unmounted.
- **Runs on disk the database doesn't know** (the database is older than the files): [scan the storage](/docs/backend-admin/storage#scanning-the-storage),
  then **Add runs to QA-Board**: they're imported again, with their metrics, from their files.
- Make the storage's snapshot and the dump close in time (the dump runs at 02:00 with docker compose and with the
  chart's default schedule) to keep these lists short.

## Practice restores {/* #drills */}
A backup you never restored is a hope. A few times a year, and after each change of your backup setup:
1. Restore the latest dump on a staging server (`deployments/deploy.py <staging env file> restore-db latest`), with
   the production encryption key.
2. Mount a restored copy of the results, or a snapshot, at the same paths.
3. Check: sign in, open a recent commit and its images, **Admin → Settings** shows no secret to fill again
   (**Security → Encryption of the stored secrets** decrypts them all).
4. Write down how long each step took: it's your RTO.

## What QA-Board doesn't do {/* #limits */}
- It doesn't back up, snapshot or replicate the results: use your storage's features (NAS snapshots, object
  storage versioning and replication, a backup agent).
- With docker compose, it doesn't copy the dumps off the host, encrypt them, or alert when they fail.
- The chart's `pg_dump` CronJob keeps no minimum number of dumps: if backups fail for `keepDays`, the last ones
  are deleted too. With CloudNativePG, configure its backups instead.
