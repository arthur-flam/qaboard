# QA-Board · Chapter 1 — From results_final_v3 to a dashboard

## chat (chat)
- Monday morning. You pushed a better denoiser. Probably better.
- Better than what? On which images? Nobody really knows.

## folders (terminal)
- Your results live in folders named by mood.
- Your metrics, in a text file you update by hand.
- And running everything again, on every image, for every change…
- Let's be honest: nobody does that.

## promise (title)
*On screen: What if every commithad its results page?*
- QA-Board tracks your algorithm's results: every commit, every input, every metric.
- Let's set it up. It takes two minutes.

## wizard (terminal)
- One command: qa wizard.
- It looks at your project: the git remote, the languages, where your test images are.
- It finds your QA-Board server…
- …and an AI assistant reads your code to call it: here, Claude, via the company gateway.
- It explores, writes the integration, and tells you what it did.
- You can talk to it, like to a colleague.
- You review everything once. Nothing is written before you say so.
- And it runs your code, for real, on one image.

## batch (terminal)
- Now every test image, in one command.
- With --share, results go to QA-Board, for the whole team.

## results (browser)
- Every input, its metrics, and the images your code wrote.
- Here, the fabric test chart: crisp, like it should be.

## change (terminal)
- Back to work. Night shots are still noisy? Smooth a bit more.
- Commit, and run the batch. Or let your CI do it.

## compare (browser)
- QA-Board compares it with the previous version.
- Night shots: much better. But look at the fabric.
- The texture is gone. You would have heard it from a customer.
- Now you know, before merging.

## outro (title)
*On screen: qa wizard*
- That's QA-Board. Run qa wizard in your project.
- Next time: tuning your algorithm's parameters, right from the browser.
